# Enumerator manual structure

Sources: Development Impact Wiki, Enumerator Training (page dated June 2024), which lists the elements of a field manual; and the Zambia school feeding enumerator field protocol (July 2026), used as a worked example. Paraphrased.

The wiki says a comprehensive manual lists: study objectives, roles and responsibilities, survey protocols (with examples that explain why each matters, for example replacing respondents), key terms, tablet instructions, a description of the questions, and FAQs.

## Rules for writing it

- Every protocol statement must come from the team, a protocol or TOR the team shared, or the wiki guidance (say so). Never invent a rule, number, date, name, or contact.
- Where the team has not said, write `[TO CONFIRM: what is needed]` and list it at the end.
- Copy consent text only from the approved consent form. Do not write it.
- Use plain, short sentences. One instruction per sentence. Explain why a protocol matters with an example.
- Keep a section only if it applies. Say which were dropped and why.

## Sections, with what to ask

| # | Section | Ask the team |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Cover table: prepared for, coverage, platform, staffing, training, version | Survey name, place, platform, version date |
| 1 | Purpose and study objectives | Objectives and outcomes in two or three sentences, in plain words |
| 2 | Operating principles (the non-negotiables) | The rules that cannot be broken, for example verify before entry, never estimate a value, keep respondent information confidential |
| 3 | Roles and responsibilities (table: who, what) | Roles in the field team, firm, and research team |
| 4 | Staffing, recruitment, and training | Team size, selection, certification rule. Link the training plan |
| 5 | Timeline and productivity standards (if used) | Calendar, daily targets, what counts as a completed case |
| 6 | Field entry and movement | Authorisation letters, courtesy calls, access, security rules, pauses |
| 7 | Survey protocols | See below |
| 8 | Tablet and form instructions | Platform, login, offline use, sync, GPS, audio audits, backup, what to do if a device fails |
| 9 | Description of the questions, module by module | See `question-guidance.md` |
| 10 | Quality checks and supervision | Completeness checks, back-checks, daily summaries, who reviews, how queries are answered |
| 11 | Final checklist before submission | The team's own checks |
| 12 | Common errors and how to avoid them | Errors seen in the pilot or earlier rounds |
| 13 | Key terms | Terms and acronyms in the form and the manual |
| 14 | Frequently asked questions | Questions from the pilot and training. Grow it after training |
| 15 | Support and escalation | Who to call for what. Ask for names and numbers, do not invent |
| Annexes | Printable field log; daily productivity and quality summary; issue or status codes; approved consent text | Which the team uses |

## Protocols to ask about (section 7)

Choose those that apply: introducing the survey and consent; who the respondent is and how they are selected within the unit; finding the respondent; refusals and absent respondents; replacement rules; revisits and call-backs; interview conduct and probing; privacy and where to interview; safety and conduct in the community; handling sensitive questions; data upload, sync, and backup; tracking sheets and status definitions; reporting problems; confidentiality and what to do with notes and photographs.

For each protocol give: the rule, an example of why it matters, and what to do when the rule cannot be followed (who to call).

## Two kinds of survey

- **Interview survey** (household, school, phone, facility, firm): sections 1 to 15. Section 7 centres on respondent selection, consent, replacement, and conduct.
- **Records retrieval** (the Zambia example): add the unit of work (for example one school-month), custody of original documents, the order of retrieval and entry, file naming, a physical field log, an electronic tracker, status definitions, and completeness checks before entry. Consent and interview conduct may not apply. Ask.

## Plain language check

Give each section a one-line summary an enumerator could repeat. Keep the tone respectful. Put the most used pages (checklist, escalation, status codes) near the front or in annexes.
