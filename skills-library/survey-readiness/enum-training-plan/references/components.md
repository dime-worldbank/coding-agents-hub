# What an enumerator training should include

Source: Development Impact Wiki, Enumerator Training (page dated June 2024) and Training Guidelines: Content and Structure (extract only). Paraphrased. Check the wiki for current content.

## Principles

- Plan the training in advance. The research team, the survey firm, and field coordinators share the work.
- The research team prepares and approves an enumerator manual. The manual is the base for the training content.
- Everyone in the field team understands the survey protocols and the questionnaire by the end.
- Always train more enumerators than the survey needs. Choose the best at the end, based on rigorous assessment.
- Train confidentiality of respondents.

## Objectives to cover

1. **Survey protocols:** pilot them before training. Examples: replacing respondents.
2. **Survey instrument:** every enumerator understands every question and can use the device (tablet or paper).
3. **Key roles:** the research team, the firm, field coordinators, supervisors, and enumerators know their duties.

## Planning

- **Logistics and recruitment (survey firm):** venue, printed questionnaires and agenda, tablets, pens and notebooks, potential enumerators and supervisors.
- **Train support staff first:** facilitators, firm managers, and supervisors learn the study context, the questionnaire, and the protocols.
- **Time frame** depends on questionnaire length and complexity (modules, repeat groups), enumerator experience, and study design complexity. Allow rest, classroom and field practice days, and at least one day to select enumerators. The wiki warns that you will never need less time in training.
- **Typical agenda items (the wiki gives):** introductions and overview of the study (about half a day); review of survey protocols (half a day to two days). Allow more for the questionnaire modules, tablet use, practice, and selection.
- **Order:** enumerators should understand the questionnaire structure and modules before using tablets.
- **Remote training:** shorter sessions over more days. One Development Impact blog reports panelists aiming for about four hours a day.

## Enumerator manual contents (see the `enum-manual` skill)

Study objectives, roles and responsibilities, survey protocols with examples, key terms, tablet instructions, description of questions, FAQs.

## Assessing enumerators

Selection criteria: scores on regular quizzes and the final test, field practice, participation, interpersonal skills, previous experience.

**Quizzes and the final test must assess:**
- understanding of the materials (manual, protocols, interviewing guidelines);
- reading skills in each survey language, including the translated questionnaire;
- understanding of questions and how to explain scales to respondents;
- numeracy and the ability to enter answers on a tablet.

**How to run them:**
- Write all quizzes and the final test before training. Edit them from what you see in the sessions.
- One quiz a day on the previous day's topics. Mark it the same day and give feedback so people can fix gaps.
- Make the final test comprehensive. Focus it on areas where people scored poorly.
- Tell enumerators in advance that there will be quizzes and a test, and which skills matter.
- Reassure them: quizzes measure progress, not only performance, and scores are one of several criteria.

## Field practice

Forms: team exercises on modules in pairs or groups; mock interviews with discussion afterwards; practice interviews with administrative officials (not in the final data), useful when the team is new to the culture and norms.

Rules: every enumerator does at least one practice interview. Assign an experienced enumerator as facilitator in each group. Facilitators take notes and give feedback at the end of each day. Before going to the field, each enumerator practises interviewing at least twice. Discard early practice interviews from the dataset.

**Observation checklist for facilitators:**
1. Uses the right equipment (tablet, or pen and notebook).
2. Selects the correct respondent.
3. Introduces the survey properly and reads the consent in the respondent's language.
4. Reads introductory sentences and all questions correctly in the respondent's language.
5. Clarifies and probes politely, at the right time.
6. Knows the survey and shows confidence.
7. Language proficiency in each survey language.
8. Interacts respectfully, verbally and non-verbally.
9. Patience and attention to detail; follows protocols.
10. Makes the respondent comfortable and reassures them about their right to privacy.

## Participation scoring

Facilitators score each enumerator from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent) on: punctuality; active participation and initiative; attitude and integrity; teamwork; communication skills.

## Best practices

- **Scientific approach:** use the same method for every group so differences are real. Keep the same process, anonymity, confidentiality, duration, discussion style, and filing for all groups.
- **Confidentiality and anonymity:** do not disclose what individuals say; use information only for the training's stated purposes; do not copy or keep identifying information; return all confidential material at the end; do not share with third parties without approval.
- **Ownership:** explain why each enumerator's role matters. Fatigue is a risk in long or travel-heavy surveys.
- Include sessions on study methodology so enumerators can handle situations the FAQs do not cover.
